The Health Benefits of True Ceylon Cinnamon vs. Cassia

When you reach for cinnamon in your spice rack, do you know which variety you are using? The distinction between true Ceylon cinnamon and common Cassia goes far beyond flavor—it is a matter of long-term health and wellness.

Understanding the Two Varieties

True Ceylon cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum), often referred to as "true" or "Sri Lankan" cinnamon, is native to the lush soils of Sri Lanka. It features soft, delicate layers that crumble easily into a fine, sweet powder. In contrast, the more commonly available Cassia cinnamon (sourced primarily from China, Indonesia, and Vietnam) has thick, hard barks that are difficult to break and possess a much sharper, pungent flavor profile.

The Crucial Difference: Coumarin Levels

The single most important health factor distinguishing these two spices is coumarin—a naturally occurring organic chemical compound that can act as a blood thinner and cause liver damage in high doses.

  • Ceylon Cinnamon: Contains negligible amounts of coumarin (typically around 0.004% or less), making it entirely safe for daily, continuous consumption.
  • Cassia Cinnamon: Contains high levels of coumarin (up to 1% or higher), which can easily exceed safe daily intake limits if you enjoy cinnamon regularly in your diet or take it for wellness supplements.

Therapeutic Properties of True Ceylon

Beyond its low coumarin safety profile, Ceylon cinnamon is celebrated for its powerful antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. It is packed with polyphenols that help protect the body from oxidative stress. Furthermore, studies suggest it supports healthy blood sugar regulation by improving insulin sensitivity, making it a favorite addition to morning teas, smoothies, and healthy bakes.

Making the Switch

If you use cinnamon daily for wellness or culinary artistry, upgrading to authentic, hand-crafted Sri Lankan grades like Alba, C4, or C5 ensures you receive the delicate fragrance and complex flavors without compromising your health.
